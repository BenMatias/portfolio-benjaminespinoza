Macroeconomic BI Case Study

U.S. Unemployment & Labor Dynamics Analytics

Macroeconomic business intelligence architecture analyzing multi-tiered labor market metrics across 50 states and 380+ metropolitan statistical areas (MSAs) using Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) series.

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Google Looker Studio Dimensional Star Schema Custom Spread Calculations (bps) State & Metro Geographic Drill-Down

The Macroeconomic Challenge

National headline unemployment rates often mask severe geographic disparities, localized industrial shocks, and uneven post-pandemic recovery speeds across state and metro economies.

The BI Analytical Solution

Built an end-to-end Looker Studio reporting platform integrating BLS time series, structured dimensional schemas, custom spread calculations (bps), and interactive cross-filtering by geographic tier.

Strategic Business Impact

Enables workforce planners, corporate economists, and policy advisors to benchmark labor market tightness, assess regional talent availability, and forecast labor cost pressures in real time.

Macroeconomic Problem & Analytical Objectives

BLS LAUS / CPS Dataset

Evaluating labor market health requires analyzing data far beyond national aggregate numbers. Aggregated headline rates fail to communicate critical regional realities: while coastal technology hubs might experience rapid labor tightening, manufacturing corridors or energy-dependent regions frequently encounter structural lag or delayed recovery.

This business intelligence project unifies multi-tiered data from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) covering the 2020–2025 period across all 50 states, Puerto Rico, and 380+ Metropolitan Statistical Areas (MSAs). The analytical goal is to empower corporate talent strategists, regional site selectors, and economic researchers with frictionless drill-down intelligence.

BI Dimensional Architecture (Star Schema)

Data Modeling Standard

To deliver sub-second query performance and seamless cross-filtering across geographic tiers in Looker Studio, the raw BLS time-series flat files were transformed into a normalized Star Schema.

Fact_Unemployment_Monthly

  • DateKey (FK) INT [YYYYMM]
  • GeoKey (FK) VARCHAR(10)
  • LaborForce BIGINT
  • EmployedCount BIGINT
  • UnemployedCount BIGINT
  • UnemploymentRate DECIMAL(5,2)
  • MoM_Change_bps FLOAT
  • YoY_Change_bps FLOAT

Dim_Geography

  • GeoKey (PK) VARCHAR(10)
  • StateName VARCHAR(50)
  • StateAbbr CHAR(2)
  • FIPS_Code CHAR(5)
  • MetroAreaName VARCHAR(100)
  • CensusRegion VARCHAR(20)
  • GeoTier ENUM(State, MSA)

Dim_Date

  • DateKey (PK) INT [YYYYMM]
  • FullDate DATE
  • Year SMALLINT
  • Quarter CHAR(2)
  • MonthName VARCHAR(15)
  • IsPostPandemic BOOLEAN

Calculated Field Logic & Spread Metrics

Calculated fields were engineered directly into the semantic layer to allow instant regional benchmarking against the national baseline: 

-- Calculated Field: State Spread to National Baseline (Basis Points)
State_Spread_bps = (AVG(UnemploymentRate) - AVG(National_UnemploymentRate)) * 100

-- Calculated Field: Volatility Index (Standard Deviation of Monthly Rate)
Regional_Volatility = STDEV(UnemploymentRate)

-- Calculated Field: Labor Tightness Classification
CASE 
    WHEN UnemploymentRate < 3.5 THEN 'Critical Tightness (High Wage Pressure)'
    WHEN UnemploymentRate BETWEEN 3.5 AND 4.5 THEN 'Balanced Labor Market'
    WHEN UnemploymentRate BETWEEN 4.5 AND 6.0 THEN 'Moderate Slack'
    ELSE 'High Underutilization'
END

Strategic Macroeconomic Findings & Recommendations

Executive Synthesis

Synthesizing the multi-year data trends across states and metropolitan statistical areas surfaces four distinct structural takeaways:

1. Sunbelt vs. Rust Belt Divergence

Sunbelt metro hubs (Texas, Florida, North Carolina) demonstrated superior labor absorption speeds post-2021, maintaining unemployment spreads 60–90 bps lower than legacy industrial corridors in the Midwest.

2. Intra-State Metro vs. Rural Disparity

High-GDP states such as California and New York exhibit extreme internal variance: tier-1 metropolitan tech centers maintain tight labor conditions, while agricultural and inland counties experience double the unemployment rate.

3. Structural Sector Resilience

States with diversified healthcare, advanced defense, and public sector employment baselines showed the lowest standard deviation in unemployment throughout economic cyclical adjustments.

4. Workforce Location Strategy

For enterprise talent acquisition, mid-sized Tier-2 MSAs (500K–1.5M population) offer the optimal balance of labor supply liquidity without the extreme wage inflation observed in Tier-1 mega-cities.
